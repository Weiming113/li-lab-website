The Li Lab

Molecular mechanisms of tissue injury and inflammation following cardiopulmonary bypass and cardiac arrest — bridging multi-omics discovery with translational cardiovascular care.

Cardiopulmonary bypass-induced inflammation and cardiac arrest-associated brain injury

The Li lab investigates the molecular mechanisms underlying tissue injury and inflammation following cardiopulmonary bypass and cardiac arrest. We integrate single-cell and spatial multi-omics, functional genomics, and clinically relevant experimental models to understand how these life-saving interventions and events reshape cellular states and regulatory programs.

The Li lab’s research interest lies on two interconnected areas: cardiac arrest-associated brain injury and cardiopulmonary bypass-induced systemic inflammation. By integrating human patient samples with animal and cellular models, we aim to identify the molecular drivers of injury, uncover new therapeutic targets, and understand how these pathological cellular programs can be reversed.

The Li lab’s ultimate goal is to translate these mechanistic discoveries into targeted therapies that reduce organ injury, promote recovery, and improve outcomes for patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery or experiencing cardiac arrest.

Our Lab Philosophy

Our lab believes that the most meaningful findings emerge from a culture of curiosity, creativity, collaboration, and intellectual freedom. We encourage every member of our lab to ask challenging questions, think independently, embrace new technologies and approaches, and explore uncharted frontiers of biology.

Most importantly, we foster an inclusive, respectful, and supportive environment where everyone is valued, all perspectives are welcomed, and every individual has the opportunity to grow and succeed.

Together, we aim to build a research community that not only advances science but also trains the next generation of scientists and transforms how we understand and treat cardiovascular disease.
